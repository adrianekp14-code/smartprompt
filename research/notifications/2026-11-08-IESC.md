# Research Notification #1 — IES Holdings (IESC)

**Status: PREPARED — HELD, NOT SENT.** IESC passed screening, primary-source verification and an independent skeptic review
("thesis survives with major caveats"). It has not yet met every notification criterion, because the FY2026 10-K is due within weeks
(third-party estimate Nov 20 – Dec 9, 2026; not confirmed by the company). That filing is needed to confirm whether the June-quarter margins
and the DBM acquisition economics hold. This note becomes a sent notification only if the checkpoints at the bottom are met.

## WHY IT IS INTERESTING
- **What it does:** IES installs electrical and technology systems through four segments:
  - Communications: data-center low-voltage and structured cabling.
  - Infrastructure Solutions: custom power-generation enclosures, switchgear and bus duct, all manufactured in-house.
  - Commercial & Industrial.
  - Residential.
  - On 2026-10-05 it added **DBM Global**, a structural-steel fabricator and erector with about $1.3B of revenue.
- **Why the niche matters:**
  - Data-center build-outs are gated by power distribution and skilled electrical labor.
  - IES combines both: it manufactures power equipment and supplies a field workforce. That gives it a scarce position at the bottleneck.
- **Growth drivers:**
  - $2.8B of contractual remaining performance obligations.
  - A further $1.7B of agreements that are not yet enforceable.
  - Capacity expansions in Infrastructure Solutions.
  - Cross-selling DBM steel packages to the same data-center and industrial customers.
- **Hard to replicate:**
  - In-house manufacturing of custom power enclosures and switchgear, with segment margins above 20%.
  - National multi-trade crews with hyperscaler-grade qualifications.
- **Track record:**
  - Diluted EPS rose from $1.55 (FY19) to $15.02 (FY25), on pre-split shares.
  - Capital allocation has been run by controlling holder Jeffrey Gendell (Tontine).
- **Why it may be overlooked now:** the stock is about 60% below its high.
  - The fall began after the debt-funded DBM deal closed: about $339 on Oct 2 to about $213 by Oct 23.
  - It was compounded by the Nov 4–5 post-midterm data-center selloff.
  - The market is pricing it as peak-cycle construction. The question is whether that discount is excessive.

## VALUATION
- **Current:**
  - Market cap is about $6.4B and EV about $6.5B.
  - EV to TTM EBIT is about 13x before DBM. It is about 11x pro forma, assuming DBM contributes roughly $80–90M of EBIT. That DBM figure is an estimate, not verified.
  - P/E on TTM earnings is roughly 16–17x.

- **Key assumptions:**
  - Margins are the swing factor. Each 1 point of margin on $5.8B is about $58M of EBIT, or about $19 per share at 13x.
  - DBM is assumed to earn a high-single-digit operating margin. Its stand-alone financials are not yet in an IES filing.

## RISKS
1. **Cycle peak.** Most segment profit depends on data-center and industrial capex. Hyperscaler spending cuts, or the post-midterm restriction
   risk Bernstein flagged as affecting about 56% of the US pipeline, would hit Communications and Infrastructure Solutions.
2. **Margin normalization.** The June quarter's short-duration jobs and securities gains will not recur. C&I could fall back toward 10–12%.
3. **Capital allocation at the top of the cycle.** DBM is a lower-margin, debt-funded steel business. Gulf Island, acquired for $152M,
   earned about $0.6M of operating income in nine months.
4. **Governance.** Tontine and Jeffrey Gendell own 51.7% and can "control most of the Company's affairs." Related-party links exist (office
   sublease, the Jett stake). A Tontine sale triggers change-of-control clauses in the credit and surety agreements.
5. **Insider selling and earnings quality.** Tontine, the CEO, the CFO and a director sold in May–Jun 2026 at about $335–382 post-split.
   Cash flow depends on customer advances, and a $311M securities portfolio adds volatility unrelated to operations.

- **Strongest argument against investing:** you would be paying 11–13x earnings that may be at a cyclical peak, in a contracting business
  that historically earned 3–5% margins. The controlling shareholder, who has sold stock, has just added debt to buy a lower-quality business.
- **What would invalidate the thesis:**
  - FY26 Q4 or FY27 C&I margins at or below about 11%.
  - Contractual RPO falling below $2.8B, or cancellations of the non-enforceable backlog.
  - DBM dragging consolidated operating margin below about 8%.
  - Negative operating cash flow as billings in excess unwind.
  - A Tontine sell-down, or further debt-funded low-margin M&A.

## FINAL ASSESSMENT
IESC deserves attention now because a large, fast drawdown has pushed a proven capital allocator with a scarce data-center power and electrical
position to roughly 11–13x EBIT. The base case is above today's price on conservative multiples. It is not a clean compounder. The risks are
cyclical, governance-related and DBM-related, and they are material. The quality of the June quarter is in doubt. The FY2026 10-K will show,
within weeks, whether margins held without the one-offs and what DBM earns. Waiting for it costs little information-wise and removes the
largest uncertainty. **Send criteria:** FY26 Q4 C&I operating margin ≥ 13%, contractual RPO ≥ $2.8B, positive operating cash flow
excluding securities purchases, and DBM economics disclosed and consistent with a high-single-digit margin.
